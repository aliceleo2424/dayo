DayO Partner Guide

How to Be a Great
Conversation Partner

◷ Reading time: about 5 minutes.

Your job is not to teach.
Your job is to make the other person feel comfortable enough to keep talking.

The DayO Partner Mindset

Listen · Ask · Wait · Share · Encourage

01Listen.Smile and listen. Ask follow-up questions and show interest in what the user is saying. 02Wait.Give them time. Silence is okay. Do not rush to answer for them. 03Share.Share your own stories. A conversation is not an interview. 04Encourage.Help them keep going. Make the next sentence feel easier, not harder.

A great DayO conversation should feel like:

“I was nervous at first, but somehow I kept talking.”

That is the experience we want to create.

Welcome to DayO 👋

DayO is a place for people to practice real conversations with real people.

As a DayO Partner, your focus is real conversation.

Your job is to help the other person feel comfortable enough to speak, make mistakes, think, laugh, and keep the conversation going.

You do not need to be a teacher.

You just need to be a warm, patient, and curious conversation partner.

Confidentiality & Privacy

Keep every conversation private.

Anything a user shares during a DayO conversation — including personal stories, workplace information, company projects, clients, products, internal plans, or business ideas — must stay confidential.

Do not record, screenshot, copy, post, quote, or share conversation content with anyone outside DayO.

Business conversation content must never be shared outside DayO.

If something needs to be reported for safety or a serious issue, use DayO’s official reporting process instead of sharing it elsewhere.

“What is shared in a DayO conversation stays in the conversation.”

01

Listen With Your Face

Listening is more than staying quiet.

Show the user that you are listening.

Examples

  • “Really?”
  • “Oh, I see.”
  • “That sounds fun!”
  • “No way!”
  • “Me too.”
  • “That makes sense.”

Avoid looking distracted, checking another screen, staying expressionless, or waiting only for your turn to speak.

The user should feel:
“This person actually wants to hear what I’m saying.”

02

Give Them Time to Think

Many DayO users are not fluent speakers.

Sometimes they already know what they want to say — they just need a few seconds to find the words.

If the user pauses:
Do not immediately answer for them.

Wait for a few seconds.
Keep a relaxed expression.

Wait before helping. Do not complete every sentence for the user. Give them time to find words, then use Wait → Hint → Example when they need help.

Useful phrases

  • “Take your time.”
  • “No rush.”
  • “It’s okay, I’m listening.”
  • “Give it a try.”
03

When the User Gets Stuck: Use the 3-Step Rule

Help without taking over. Start with the smallest amount of help they need.

  1. STEP 1 — WAIT

    Give them a few seconds.

    • Smile
    • Keep a warm, attentive expression
    • Nod / react naturally
    • Show clearly that you're listening

    Useful phrases

    • “Take your time.”
    • “No rush.”
    • “I'm listening.”

  2. STEP 2 — GIVE A SMALL HINT

    If they still cannot continue, give one key word or just the beginning of a sentence.

    Example

    User“Yesterday I… um…”

    Partner“Went to…?”

    Or try

    “You can start with, ‘I usually…’”

    Do not finish the sentence for them. Leave room for the user to continue in their own words.

  3. STEP 3 — GIVE A FULL EXAMPLE

    If they are still completely stuck, offer one complete example sentence.

    “You could say,
    ‘I usually go there with my friends on weekends.’”

    Then give the user a chance to speak again right away:
    “How about you?”

Wait → Hint → Example

Not

User pauses → Partner answers for them.

If the question itself is too difficult, ask it in an easier way.

Instead of

“What aspect of Korean culture surprised you the most?”

Try

“Was anything surprising when you came to Korea?”

04

Help them speak better — without turning it into a lesson

DayO is about conversation confidence, not perfect grammar.

Correct naturally, then keep the conversation moving.

Let the user finish speaking. If the meaning is clear, do not interrupt mid-sentence. Respond naturally using the correct version, then continue with a reaction or follow-up question.

Do not repeatedly stop the user to explain grammar unless they explicitly ask.

Example 1

User“I no have dog.”

Good“Oh, you don’t have a dog? I don’t have a dog either.”

Avoid

“No. You should say, ‘I don’t have a dog.’”

Example 2

User“Yesterday I go shopping.”

Good“Oh, you went shopping yesterday? What did you buy?”

Example 3

User“She very kind.”

Good“Yeah, she’s very kind. How did you meet her?”

Example 4

User“I like travel Korea.”

Good“You like traveling in Korea? Which city do you like most?”

This gives the user the correct expression while keeping the conversation natural.

Example

User“Yesterday I go to Hongdae.”

Do not interrupt with

“No, you should say ‘went.’”

Instead

“Oh, you went to Hongdae yesterday? What did you do there?”

Stop to explain a correction only when:

  • the user asks
  • the meaning is unclear
  • the same mistake keeps blocking communication

Goal: More speaking, less fear.

05

Share Your Own Stories

A good conversation goes both ways.

Ask plenty of follow-up questions so the user has more chances to speak.

But don't turn the conversation into an interview.

Avoid this rhythm

Question → answer → new unrelated question.

Better

User“I like cooking.”

Partner“Oh, what do you like to cook?”
“I love pasta, so I cook it a lot too.”

Share your own reactions, experiences, and small stories too.

Bad

“What food do you like?”
“How often do you eat it?”
“Who do you eat with?”
“Where do you usually eat?”

Better

User“I like cooking pasta.”

Partner“Oh, nice! What kind of pasta do you usually make?
I make aglio e olio a lot because it’s easy,
but I always add too much garlic.”

Follow up and share

User“I like traveling.”

Partner“Really? Where did you go most recently?”

User“Japan.”

Partner“Nice! What did you enjoy most?
I went to Osaka once and spent way too much money on food.”

Useful rhythm:

Ask → Listen → React → Share → Ask again

06

Use the Topic Cards, But Don’t Read Them Like a Script

DayO provides conversation cards to help you.

You do not need to:

  • ask every question
  • follow the exact order
  • finish every card

If the conversation becomes interesting, stay with it.

One good topic can easily become a 10-minute conversation.

Follow the conversation, not the script.

07

When the User Gives Very Short Answers

If the user answers:

  • “Yes.”
  • “No.”
  • “Maybe.”
  • “I don’t know.”

Make the question smaller.

Instead of

“Tell me about your favorite trip.”

Try

“Have you traveled outside Korea?”
“Where did you go?”
“What did you like there?”

You can also share first.

“I went to Busan recently and ate way too much seafood.
Have you been there?”

08

If They Use Their Native Language

That is okay.

If they briefly use Korean or another language,
help them return to the target language.

Example

User“그거 영어로 뭐라고 하지…”

Partner“You mean ‘crowded’?”

User“Yes! It was very crowded.”

Partner“Exactly. So where was it crowded?”

Help → return → continue.

Keep explanations brief unless the user asks for more.

09

Keep the Conversation Safe and Professional

All DayO conversations should stay on the DayO platform.

Do not ask users for:

  • phone numbers
  • Instagram IDs
  • KakaoTalk IDs
  • private messaging accounts
  • private meetings

Do not make romantic or sexual comments.
Do not pressure users to share personal information.

Friendly is good.
Personal boundaries are also important.

10

A Simple Session Flow

Beginning

Make them comfortable.

Examples

“Hi! Nice to meet you.”
“How are you feeling today?”
“Don’t worry about making mistakes. We’re just going to talk.”

The user should understand:
This is not a test.

Middle

Use topic cards.
Ask. React. Share. Wait.
Stay with good topics.

Ending

Finish positively.

Examples

“I really enjoyed talking with you.”
“You told that story really well.”
“You spoke a lot today!”
“It was fun talking with you.”
“See you again on DayO!”

Write a Letter that proves you listened

A good Partner Letter should contain something that could only have been written after this specific conversation.

Write 2–4 short sentences and mention at least one real detail from today. You can add a recommendation, encouragement, something you remembered, or something to talk about next time.

Too generic

“It was nice talking to you. Have a great day!”

This could be sent to anyone and does not feel personal.

A personal Letter

“I loved hearing about the café near your house.
You said you want to visit Busan this winter — I hope you get to go!
Next time, I’d love to hear how your trip planning is going.”

Encouragement with a real detail

“You kept talking even when you needed time to find the words today.
I remember you said you love horror movies — I recommend Train to Busan if you haven’t seen it yet!”

Sentence starters

  • I really enjoyed hearing about...
  • I remember you mentioned...
  • Since you said you like..., I recommend...
  • You kept the conversation going even when...
  • Next time, I’d love to hear more about...
  • Hope you get to...

Do not copy a starter without adding a real detail from the conversation.

Before every conversation

  • Check the Conversation Brief
  • Check mic/camera
  • Open Talk Cards
  • Remember: let the user speak first
  • Correct naturally, not like a teacher
  • Keep the conversation confidential
  • Write a personal Letter afterward

The feeling that matters

What We Want the User to Feel

“I was nervous, but I actually talked.”
“I made mistakes, but it was okay.”
“That felt easier than I expected.”
“I want to do it again.”

That is a successful DayO session.

Keep this close

Quick Do / Don’t

✓ Do

  • Smile and react
  • Give the user time
  • Ask simple follow-up questions
  • Share your own experiences
  • Keep the atmosphere relaxed
  • Let mistakes happen
  • Help only as much as needed
  • End positively

− Don’t

  • Correct every sentence
  • Finish every sentence for the user
  • Ask questions nonstop
  • Over-explain instead of conversing
  • Show impatience during silence
  • Judge their level
  • Ask for private contact information
  • Make the conversation romantic or uncomfortable

The One Rule to Remember

Make it easier for them to say the next sentence.

You do not need to teach perfectly.
You do not need to entertain constantly.

Just help the other person feel safe enough to keep talking.

That is what makes a great DayO Partner.
